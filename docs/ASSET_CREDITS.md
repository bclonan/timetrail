# Example asset credits

Generated on 2026-09-02 with the built-in image generation tool. Original files
were copied into `public/example-assets`. They are artist concepts, not evidence
or photographs of actual products. The calling agent used these prompts:

- `europa-orbit.png`: wide cinematic view of Europa's cracked icy surface with
  Jupiter in the distance, physically plausible sunlight, rust-orange fractures,
  dark navy and pearl colors, clear space for a headline, no text or spacecraft.
- `europa-ocean.png`: wide speculative view below Europa's ice, blue-white ice
  ceiling, dim ocean, rocky seafloor and subtle mineral plumes, no life forms or
  vehicles, no text. The film explicitly labels this interior as imagined.
- `atlas.png`: wide product image of a fictional graphite expedition backpack
  with a slim integrated solar panel and small amber indicator, dark studio
  lighting, backpack on the right, no text or trademarks.

`europa-cutaway.svg` is a TimeTrail schematic, not to scale. It illustrates the
layers described in [NASA's Europa facts](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/europa/europa-facts/).

`europa-nasa.mp4` is NASA's [What You Need to Know About Europa](https://images.nasa.gov/details/What%20You%20Need%20to%20Know%20About%20Europa),
the mobile MP4 variant downloaded through the NASA image and video library.
The item credits producers Jessie Wilde and Scott Bednar, editor Matt Schara,
and NASA. The source's description predates launch; the film does not repeat
its outdated launch forecast. NASA's media guidelines and item credits apply.
The film's other source notes link to the [mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/)
and [ocean evidence](https://europa.nasa.gov/europa/ocean/).

Mars, pizza and phishing examples use authored generated title cards. They do
not claim that image generation or real-world research ran when a user clicks
Create example draft. Atlas specifications and energy numbers are fictional.
